Poster Figures -- SEED symposium poster
=========================================

Figures built specifically for the poster (screenshots/pygame/ holds
the general project screenshots this folder is split out from).
Naming convention: figureN_<description>.png, generated by
generate_figureN_<description>.py in this same folder. poster_common.py
holds the shared field-drawing helpers figure5 and figure9 both use.

The numbers are stable FILE IDs, not the poster's reading order --
figures 1-4 were built first and 5-9 added later. Suggested placement
on the poster, which is a different order:

    Background   figure5 (pose vs. obstacle error)
    Approach     figure1 (field + algorithms)   [Pillar 1]
                 figure6 (match lifecycle)      [Pillar 3]
                 figure9 (deviation types)      [Pillar 3]
    Results      figure2 (reliability/dollar)   [was Pillar 4]
                 figure3 (bundle scatter)       [was Pillar 5]
                 figure4 (scenario deep-dive)   [was Pillar 5]
                 figure7 (sensor coverage)      -- explains fig2/fig3
                 figure8 (severity curves)

That is 9 figures and a typical poster carries 3-6. figure5 + figure7
are the two highest-value additions to the original four; figure6,
figure8 and figure9 are reserves for dead space. figure9's middle panel
duplicates figure5's right panel, so those two are alternatives more
than companions.


figure1_ftc_field.png -- FTC field + A*-planned path + algorithm comparison
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure1_ftc_field.py, not captured from an
interactive session like screenshots/pygame's scenarios. Two panels:

  Left  -- an actual FTC-scale (144in x 144in, 6in cells = 24x24) grid
           from ftc.field.build_grid("cluttered"), planned with
           nav.algorithms.astar, robot drawn mid-route with its real
           rotated footprint (the same corner math ftc/field.py's
           collision check uses) over the *eroded* (real, uninflated)
           obstacle cells.
  Right -- Dijkstra vs A* vs RRT vs RRT* planning-time/quality stats,
           recomputed live from benchmark_results/results.csv
           (nav/benchmark.py's 20-trial run) -- the comparison Pillar 1
           cites for why A* is the algorithm every match in this
           project plans with. Plus a fifth, bar-less D* Lite row (see
           below).

The D* Lite row. The panel is titled "5 pathfinding algorithms tested"
but only four have bars, because D* Lite is timed on a different
quantity: repairing a route after the world changes, not planning one
from scratch (nav/algorithms.py's own dstar_lite branch makes the same
point -- one find_path call can't show what the algorithm is for).
Putting a repair cost on the same log axis as four planning costs would
invite a comparison neither number supports, so it gets a labeled row,
an em dash, and its real numbers in the note instead.

Those numbers are recomputed from benchmark_results/replan_results.csv,
so re-running nav/replan_benchmark.py and then regenerating this figure
is all it takes to keep them current. (replan_writeup.md used to be
hand-maintained beside a machine-generated CSV and drifted badly enough
to claim 2.42x where the run gave ~1x; nav/replan_benchmark.py now
generates that writeup too.)

These are wall-clock timings and the 25x25 figures move run to run --
the moving-obstacle number lands near 1.0x either side. Treat the row
as "a tie at field scale, a clear loss on sensor discovery," which is
what's stable, rather than as two precise constants.

Regenerate after any change to ftc/field.py, ftc/match.py, or the
footprint/collision geometry those two share, or after re-running
nav/benchmark.py or nav/replan_benchmark.py:

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure1_ftc_field.py"


figure2_reliability_per_dollar.png -- which sensor is the best buy?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure2_reliability_per_dollar.py. Success-rate gain
over the free DeadReckoningSuite baseline, per $100 spent, at
variance_level >= 0.3 across all 3 deviation types -- recomputed live
from benchmark_results/ftc_suite_results.csv (ftc/suite_benchmark.py's
headline sweep) rather than hardcoded. Bars are sorted best-value-first
and self-labeled with cost + raw success rate, a readability rework of
ftc/suite_benchmark.py's own plot_reliability_per_dollar chart (and an
earlier FTC-Sensor-Study-Poster draft's "Figure 1") -- see the
docstring at the top of the script for the specific problems that
rework fixes.

Regenerate after re-running ftc/suite_benchmark.py (i.e. whenever
benchmark_results/ftc_suite_results.csv changes):

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure2_reliability_per_dollar.py"


figure3_bundle_scatter.png -- every buildable robot: cost vs. success
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure3_bundle_scatter.py. Cost vs. weighted success
rate (across 5 scenario profiles) for all 23 candidates in
benchmark_results/ftc_optimizer_results.csv (ftc/optimizer.py's bundle
search: individual sensors AND every buildable 2-3 sensor bundle),
deduplicated to 12 visually distinct (cost, success) points -- every
"+IMU" bundle variant lands on the exact same point as its non-IMU
twin, since IMU is free and changes neither cost nor measured success
here, so plotting all 23 raw points would silently overplot 11 of the
12 real positions. All 12 points are labeled (not just the 4-point
Pareto frontier), individual-reachable points are squares and
bundle-only points are circles. A readability rework of
ftc_optimizer_frontier.png's left panel (and an earlier
FTC-Sensor-Study-Poster draft's "Figure 3" left panel).

Regenerate after re-running ftc/optimizer_benchmark.py (i.e. whenever
benchmark_results/ftc_optimizer_results.csv changes):

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure3_bundle_scatter.py"


figure4_scenario_deepdive.png -- which robot wins YOUR scenario?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure4_scenario_deepdive.py, same source CSV as
figure3. For each of the 5 scenario profiles: the best scenario-specific
robot (individual sensor or bundle, whichever wins, ties broken toward
the cheaper option) vs. figure3's Pareto-best "generalist" bundle
(odometry pods + front camera, $304.99) evaluated on that same
scenario. They tie in 4 of 5 scenarios -- the generalist already is
scenario-optimal there -- except Heavy pose drift, where a cheaper,
scenario-specific bundle measurably beats it. Color coding matches
figure3 (orange = single sensor, blue = bundle, green = the frontier/
generalist pick). A synthesis of an earlier FTC-Sensor-Study-Poster
draft's "Figure 3" right panel and "Figure 4".

Regenerate after re-running ftc/optimizer_benchmark.py (i.e. whenever
benchmark_results/ftc_optimizer_results.csv changes):

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure4_scenario_deepdive.py"


figure5_pose_vs_obstacle_error.png -- the two failure modes
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure5_pose_vs_obstacle_error.py. Two panels on the
real ftc.field grid, with deviations produced by
nav.field_variance.generate_ground_truth rather than drawn by hand.
Left: the robot's BELIEVED pose tracks the planned route while its TRUE
pose drifts off it (the believed robot is the one on the route -- it is
executing its plan correctly in its own frame; drawing it the other way
round would invert the mechanism). Right: map/field mismatch, with the
one added obstacle that actually blocks the route called out.

The right panel's seed is chosen, not arbitrary: the unplanned blocker
is probabilistic, so most seeds put none on the route and a few put
two. See the script's comment.

Regenerate after any change to ftc/field.py or nav/field_variance.py:

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure5_pose_vs_obstacle_error.py"


figure6_match_lifecycle.png -- how one simulated match works
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure6_match_lifecycle.py. Pure schematic (no CSV, no
simulation): PLAN -> DRIVE -> SENSE -> RE-PLAN? on one line with the
repeat arc above it, and all three terminal outcomes fanning out of a
single check point. That single check point is the faithful picture --
ftc/match.py tests all three conditions after every step, so hanging
"success" off the planning box (as an earlier draft did) implied a
control flow the code doesn't have.

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure6_match_lifecycle.py"


figure7_sensor_coverage.png -- why distance sensors underperform
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure7_sensor_coverage.py. The ~75-degrees-of-360
coverage of the 3-sensor distance suite, computed from ftc/config.py
(DISTANCE_SENSOR_COUNT, DISTANCE_SENSOR_HALF_ANGLE_DEG,
DISTANCE_SENSOR_MOUNT_HEADINGS_DEG, DISTANCE_SENSOR_RANGE_CELLS) rather
than drawn to taste -- change the config and the figure changes. This is
the mechanism behind the negative bar in figure2 and the worst point in
figure3.

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure7_sensor_coverage.py"


figure8_severity_curves.png -- success rate vs. deviation severity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure8_severity_curves.py from
benchmark_results/ftc_suite_results.csv. The only poster figure showing
DEGRADATION rather than a single-point summary. A deliberate
simplification of benchmark_results/ftc_suite_comparison.png, which
plots all 7 suites with CI bands in 3 stacked panels -- correct, but
unreadable at poster size. Here: 3 suites (free baseline / best value /
best raw), no CI bands, panels side by side.

Regenerate after re-running ftc/suite_benchmark.py:

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure8_severity_curves.py"


figure9_deviation_types.png -- the three deviation axes
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built by generate_figure9_deviation_types.py. Three field thumbnails,
each turning ON exactly one deviation axis with the other two held at 0
-- the same isolation ftc/suite_benchmark.py's sweep uses, so these are
the literal experimental conditions rather than illustrations of them.
The blocker panel's seed is chosen because the blocker is probabilistic
(see the script's comment); a panel captioned "something parks on the
planned route" showing an empty route would be misleading.

Overlaps figure5 -- see the placement note at the top of this file.

    python3 "screenshots/poster figures/generate_figure9_deviation_types.py"
