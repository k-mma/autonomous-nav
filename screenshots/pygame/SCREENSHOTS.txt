Pygame 3-Panel Visualizer -- Screenshot Guide
================================================

Seven screenshots, one per preset scenario in scenarios/, each run
through the same 3-panel comparison tool (Dijkstra | A* | RRT) so the
three algorithms are always shown solving the exact same map side by
side. Every panel's explored cells, path, and cost are computed live
by this project's actual planning code -- these aren't mockups. (RRT's
own numbers can vary a little run to run -- it's the one algorithm here
that samples randomly rather than searching systematically, so its
exact node/cost count isn't perfectly reproducible the way the other
two panels' are; that's a real property of RRT, not a rendering bug.)

Every scenario now scatters bush, mud, and/or water across most of its
map rather than one small patch, and every cell's terrain color is
always drawn at full strength -- a path or explored cell is drawn as a
smaller solid-color square inset over the full terrain-colored cell,
leaving a visible terrain-colored border around it, instead of the two
colors being mixed together. An earlier version blended path/explored
color into the terrain color, which technically kept some terrain
visible but visually washed out the difference between terrain types
once mixed with a strongly-saturated overlay (bush, mud, and water all
blend toward nearly the same muddy yellow under enough path-yellow).
Keeping both colors at full saturation -- terrain in the border,
overlay in the center -- means every terrain type stays as easy to
identify under a busy Dijkstra wavefront as it is on open ground.


forest_corridor.png -- "Forest Corridor"
-------------------------------------------
Two walls, each with a single narrow gap, force all three algorithms
through the identical pair of chokepoints -- there's no room for one
algorithm to simply find an easier route than another. Bush, mud, and
water are scattered across the whole map now (not just flanking the
gaps, though that deliberate bush border is still there), each staying
fully visible as a colored ring around every explored or path cell that
crosses it. Dijkstra floods the whole reachable map (243 cells) before
settling on the shortest path; A* reaches the same 21.0 cost while
exploring 200 fewer cells (43) by always expanding toward the goal
first. RRT finds a valid but longer route by random sampling, the
tradeoff for not doing a systematic search at all.


costed_clearing.png -- "Costed Clearing"
--------------------------------------------
The one scenario with obstacle-inflation cost-map weighting (K) and
diagonal movement both switched on, with bush and mud scattered
broadly plus a dedicated water strip across the middle. The water
strip stays recognizably blue-gray even where Dijkstra and A*'s
explored cells cover it. The peach halo around every obstacle is the
inflation cost pushing routes away from walls, clearest in the RRT
panel where cells aren't bordered by an explored overlay at all;
diagonal movement lets Dijkstra and A* cut corners instead of
stair-stepping. Dijkstra and A* land on the identical 76.1-cost route;
RRT's route costs noticeably more since it isn't optimizing for the
weighted cost field, only for a route that's collision-free.


deep_forest.png -- "Deep Forest"
------------------------------------
A generated maze -- by construction, exactly one route exists between
any two cells -- with mud and bush filling most of its dead-end
branches (alternating the two so neighboring dead ends are easy to
tell apart, not just "some terrain in here somewhere"). This is the
scenario built to make RRT struggle: with only one true path through
the corridors, random sampling repeatedly lands in dead ends and has
to backtrack, visible as the tangle of short green tree edges doubling
back on themselves. Dijkstra and A* instead follow the maze's single
corridor directly and land on the same 40.0-cost path, A* reaching it
after exploring 23 fewer cells (48 vs. 71).


open_field.png -- "Open Field"
------------------------------------
Sparse, scattered obstacles on otherwise open ground, now with bush,
mud, and water scattered across the entire field plus a denser bush/
mud patch near the center -- this went from the sparsest-terrain
scenario in the set to one of the richest, since "open ground" doesn't
have to mean "empty of terrain," just empty of anything that forces a
particular route. Dijkstra's explored region is a near-perfect radial
blob (361 cells) that fully covers the terrain, all of it still
identifiable underneath; A* reaches the identical 36.0-cost path while
only touching 121 cells, visibly narrower and aimed at the goal; RRT's
tree sprawls in every direction before finding a serviceable but
longer route.


search_snapshot.png -- "Search Snapshot" (step mode)
---------------------------------------------------------
The other six scenarios all show a finished search. This one is frozen
mid-expansion instead, 60 steps into each algorithm's run, over a map
with bush, mud, and water scattered across nearly all of it -- a frozen
wavefront is exactly where terrain just ahead of (and bordering) the
revealed cells is easiest to actually see, since the eye can compare
"revealed, terrain visible at the edge" against "not yet revealed,
terrain in full" side by side. Dijkstra's wavefront is still a diamond
expanding outward (60/370 cells revealed), A*'s frontier is already
angled toward the goal (60/132), and RRT's tree is already complete at
this point (29/29 nodes) -- illustrating that RRT's "search" and
"result" are much closer together in time than a grid search's.


hidden_animals.png -- "Hidden Animals"
--------------------------------------------
A robot walking its planned route with a simulated lidar sensor
(magenta ring) instead of a perfect map, across a forest floor of
bush, mud, and water -- solid black cells are obstacles the sensor has
actually detected; faint outlined cells are obstacles the robot
doesn't know about yet. Five moving "animals" wander the map, and one
has already been spotted (orange) inside the sensor ring. This
particular frame catches the robot mid-replan -- the status line reads
"REPLANNING...", meaning a newly-sensed or moving obstacle just landed
on its route and a fresh path is being computed right now, not just a
robot calmly walking a route it already knows is clear.


unreliable_sensor.png -- "Unreliable Sensor"
--------------------------------------------------
The same idea as Hidden Animals, but the sensor itself is now
imperfect -- missed detections, position-jittered readings, and
occasional false positives -- crossing a heavier, slightly different
terrain mix (bush/mud/water vs. Hidden Animals' bush/mud/water at
different densities) so the two sensor scenarios don't look identical.
The status line's "22 raw / 8 confirmed" is the point of this
scenario: raw sensor reports are noisy enough that they can't be
trusted individually, so the robot only acts on a cell once it's been
reported multiple times, the same "don't replan on a single suspicious
reading" caution a real noisy sensor demands.
